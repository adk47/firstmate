# B2B Ads Email Analysis, Lessons from Real Outreach

## Dataset
~40 cold emails sent from a@muso.ai, labeled "B2B - Ads", Feb-Mar 2026.
Contacts: VP/Director/CMO-level at pro audio, music tech, education, and gear companies.

## What Got Replies (and Why)

### Mix With The Masters (Luke Buneo, Head of Digital)
- **Result:** Reply in hours → call booked next day → contract negotiation → campaign submitted
- **Why it worked:** MWTM's entire business is teaching mixing/production. Muso's 41K mixing engineers = their exact audience. The match was obvious.
- **Lesson:** When the ICP fit is self-evident, you barely need to explain it.

### SSL / Audiotonix (Martin Bennett, Group Marketing Director)
- **Result:** Same-day reply, added 2 colleagues → proposal → multi-brand deal (SSL + Slate Digital)
- **Why it worked:** Martin oversees marketing for multiple pro audio brands. One partnership = multiple brands. He saw leverage.
- **Lesson:** Identify when one contact unlocks multiple brands/budgets.

### Casio (Rakshak Kumar, Director Brand Strategy)
- **Result:** Replied to follow-up → call → sent product list for campaigns
- **Why it worked:** Casio Musical Instruments needs to reach musicians who actually buy. Muso's verified base beats generic music interest targeting.
- **Lesson:** Follow-up converted here. Persistence works when the value prop is clear.

### Oeksound (Atte Karm, Marketing)
- **Result:** "I'm definitely intrigued, it'd be great to hear more", same day
- **Why it worked:** Small plugin company. Getting in front of 500K verified producers/engineers is worth more to them than a big brand.
- **Lesson:** Smaller brands reply faster because the opportunity feels disproportionately valuable.

### EarPeace (Jay Clark, Founder)
- **Result:** "This could be interesting... send whatever deck and ask you have"
- **Why it worked:** Used a slightly more casual, evolved template. Jay is a founder, peer energy resonated.
- **Lesson:** Founder-to-founder tone gets responses from other founders.

### GlowBright/Pearl (Federico Cappuccilli)
- **Result:** Replied in 60 seconds, call next day
- **Why it worked:** Most personalized email of the batch. Mentioned specific brand (Pearl), mutual connection, and "caught our attention."
- **Lesson:** Even minimal personalization dramatically increases reply speed.

## What Got Nothing

~20+ contacts received the identical template with only company name swapped:
- No company-specific hook or insight
- No reason why THEIR brand specifically should care
- Generic "exploring partnerships" language
- Follow-ups were also identical templates

```

spots are filled, 2 left.

The brands already in get first-mover access to hyper-target 500K+ verified 
music professionals by role, credits, genre, streams, and career stage. 
No other platform can do this.

I really think [Company] should be in this launch group. Didn't want the 
window to close without reaching out one more time.
```

## Key Stats (Use in Copy)
- 500K+ verified music professionals
- 86% of the top 1% of songwriters, producers, engineers, and artists
- Segmentation: role, credits, genre, career stage, streams, geography
- ~41K mixing engineers, ~272K US-based, ~36K UK-based
- Credit-recency targeting (last 90 days = actively working)
- Not available on any other ad platform, completely unique inventory

## What to Fix (Nick's Direction)
1. **Shorter.** Original was 5 paragraphs. Cut to 3 max.
2. **More confident.** No "exploring partnerships" or "we're looking to." State what you have.
3. **Intriguing.** Don't explain everything. Make them want to ask.
4. **CEO energy.** Warm, direct, personal. Not a sales sequence.
5. **Company specific hook.** Use Company Intelligence data to show you've done homework.
6. **No identical templates.** Every email must have at least one line that couldn't apply to any other company.
